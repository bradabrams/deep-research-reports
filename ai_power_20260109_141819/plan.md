# Research Plan: AI Power Consumption & Energy Requirements

## Topic Interpretation & Assumptions

**Primary Interpretation:** This research focuses on the **energy consumption and power requirements of artificial intelligence systems** - including training large models, inference operations, data center infrastructure, and the broader implications for energy grids and sustainability.

### Key Assumptions
1. "AI power" refers to electrical power/energy consumption rather than AI capabilities or influence
2. The scope includes both training and inference workloads
3. Focus is on modern AI systems (2020-2025), particularly large language models and generative AI
4. Geographic scope is global, with emphasis on major AI development regions (US, China, EU)
5. Both current state and near-term projections (2025-2030) are relevant

---

## Research Questions

### Technical/Factual Core (Hardware & Infrastructure)

1. **What is the current estimated global electricity consumption of AI systems, and how has this grown since 2020?**
   - Sub-questions: Training vs. inference split, data center share

2. **How much energy does training a large language model (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Llama) consume, and how does this compare to everyday activities?**
   - Metrics: kWh, CO2 equivalent, household comparisons

3. **What is the energy consumption breakdown between AI training and AI inference at scale?**
   - Industry estimates suggest inference may dominate total consumption

4. **What hardware innovations (GPUs, TPUs, custom ASICs) are improving AI energy efficiency, and by how much?**
   - NVIDIA H100/B200 efficiency gains, Google TPU evolution

5. **How do different AI architectures (transformers, diffusion models, mixture-of-experts) compare in energy efficiency?**

### Economic/Business Implications

6. **What are the projected costs of powering AI infrastructure through 2030, and who bears these costs?**
   - Cloud providers, enterprises, end users

7. **How are major AI companies (OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Meta, Anthropic) addressing their energy demands?**
   - Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs), renewable commitments, nuclear investments

8. **What is the economic impact of AI power demands on electricity prices and grid infrastructure investments?**

9. **How does the cost of energy influence where AI data centers are located geographically?**
   - Nordic countries, hydropower regions, emerging markets

10. **What is the business case for on-premise vs. cloud AI from an energy cost perspective?**

### Environmental & Sustainability Perspectives

11. **What is the carbon footprint of the AI industry, and how does it compare to other major industries (aviation, cryptocurrency)?**

12. **What renewable energy strategies are AI companies pursuing, and are they sufficient to meet net-zero goals?**

13. **How do water consumption requirements for AI data center cooling impact local environments and communities?**

14. **What role can nuclear power (including SMRs) play in meeting AI energy demands?**
   - Microsoft/Constellation deal, Amazon/Talen Energy, Google/Kairos

### Historical Context & Trends

15. **How has AI energy consumption evolved from early neural networks to modern foundation models?**
   - Historical growth rates, inflection points

16. **What lessons from cryptocurrency mining energy debates apply to AI power consumption?**

17. **How do current AI power projections compare to past predictions, and what were the accuracy patterns?**

### Critical Perspectives & Risks

18. **What are the geopolitical implications of AI power concentration in specific regions or companies?**

19. **Could AI power demands create energy access inequities between developed and developing nations?**

20. **What are the risks of AI power demand outpacing grid capacity, and what blackout/brownout scenarios exist?**

## Research Methodology

### Source Categories
1. **Academic Papers**: Nature, Science, ACM, IEEE publications
2. **Industry Reports**: IEA, Bloomberg NEF, Goldman Sachs, McKinsey
3. **Company Disclosures**: Sustainability reports, earnings calls, press releases
4. **Government Data**: EIA, EPA, EU energy agencies
5. **Investigative Journalism**: NYT, WSJ, The Guardian, Wired, MIT Technology Review

### Confidence Framework
- **HIGH**: 3+ independent credible sources agreeing
- **MEDIUM**: 2 sources or credible estimates with caveats
- **LOW**: Single source or significant uncertainty
- **DISPUTED**: Conflicting credible sources
